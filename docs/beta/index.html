Coming from Cold Turkey or Freedom

They block a few apps and sites.
This blocks the machine.

  • Cold TurkeyA list
  • FreedomA list
  • FocusMeA list
  • LeechBlockOne browser
  • StayFocusdOne browser
  • The All BlockMouse + keyboard

Every other blocker operates on a list — sites, apps, or a list you’re expected to constantly update. Lists have limitations, and the urge to escape is remarkably adept at finding those gaps: the app you missed adding, the second browser, the phone hotspot, or the setting you can still reach because your keyboard still works.

The All Block doesn’t use a list at all because it isn’t blocking anything. Instead, it disables the mouse and the keyboard for the duration of the session. Not just the browser — the entire computer. There’s no app it overlooked, no window you can click into, because clicking itself has been disabled.

It seals the loopholes those apps leave open. It runs as an administrator, so nothing else can shut it down. It survives a reboot and reappears locked. Task Manager, Alt+Tab, and Ctrl+Alt+Del can’t break through.

Then the schedule removes the final barrier — you. A block that activates automatically at 09:00 was never a choice you made, and the urge to escape lives entirely within that decision. Place the Accountability Lock at the top, and even you can’t undo it.

Accountability Lock · free in every copy

Hand the key to somebody else.

A schedule only lasts until the person it’s meant to stop opens Settings and deletes it. So set a password, and let a parent, a partner, a friend — or your own steadier self, twenty minutes ago — be the one who holds it.

  • Blocks can't be edited or deleted
  • Blocks can't be switched off
  • The All Block can't be closed or exited
  • It rearms itself at every restart
  • Turning it back on never needs the password

One thing it deliberately leaves alone: the monthly emergency unlock. A lock with no exit at all is how somebody ends up unable to answer a phone call — so the way out stays limited and intentional, rather than vanishing entirely.

FAQ

If something goes sideways.

The questions that actually come in — and the way out of each one.

Still stuck? Email me directly — it's a one-person project, so it's me who answers.

A session started on its own, or in the middle of the night?

That's a scheduled block firing. Scheduled blocks run at their set time whether the app is open or not, and on a laptop they can wake the machine to start on time. Open The All Block → Schedule to change or remove one — a session you start by hand never begins on its own.

I'm stuck on the lock screen and nothing responds.

Press Ctrl Alt Delete and end The All Block from Task Manager. Windows reserves that combination system-wide, so the lock screen can never intercept it — it's always your way back in. To end the session properly instead, hold Esc for five seconds to spend a monthly emergency unlock.

How many emergency unlocks do I get?

One a month free, three a month on Pro, refilling on the 1st. Need another the same month, an extra Escape is £0.99 and never expires. Hold Esc for five seconds on the lock screen to use one.

Does closing the app or restarting my PC end a session?

No — it re-arms the same session on the way back, down to the time that was left. Ctrl Alt Delete → Task Manager is still there if you genuinely need out before the timer.

Is there a subscription?

No. Manual sessions and the Accountability Lock are free forever, and Pro is a single £12.99 payment that never renews. There's nothing to cancel.

Is the Android app available?

Yes. Get the Android companion on Google Play for manual timers, whole-phone blocking and schedules that sync with Windows.

Where does my data live?

On your machine. Session history, streaks and the Accountability Lock password never leave it. An account is optional — it exists so Pro survives a reinstall, and so schedules can reach the Android companion.

My touchpad feels off after restarting during a break.

The All Block disables some touchpad gestures during a session and restores them after; a restart mid-session can interrupt that hand-back. The app repairs it on its next launch, or fix it now in Windows Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Touchpad → “Reset touchpad settings and gestures to defaults.”

First run

Windows will warn you.

The All Block isn't code-signed yet, and SmartScreen warns about anything unsigned no matter what's in it. So you'll see this once, on the first launch. Here's the way through it.

  1. Click “More info” The warning says Windows doesn't recognise the publisher — not that it found anything wrong.
  2. Click “Run anyway” The button only appears after the first step.
  3. Click “Yes” on the admin prompt Admin is what lets The All Block hold the input lock against everything else. Without it, some of the blocking is skipped.

About

Why this exists.

The Developer's story

I was so addicted to video games and social media, that I regularly found myself awake in the late hours of the night, 3 or 4 am, gaming or watching some useless YouTube or Instagram video. I would be tired the next day, with barely enough energy to make it through the a day's work, study or to go to the gym.

I tried different app blockers to help control my screen time but they never really worked, because it was quite easy to bypass them if I was determined. And if they did, they would be aufully confusing, expensive, with annoying monthly subscriptions, and crazy prices to own any of them. Also, they would often only work on a single device, with a separate app for each device, which was a pain to manage.

That's why I set out to make my own app that would remedy all these issues at once. Windows and android are out, and macOS and iOS are coming soon.

The entire project was made, funded and maintained by myself, no team or investors, so your support would mean a ton!

Got an idea or spotted a bug? Email me — I'd love to hear it!
